# fx-hedgehog

An FX treasury risk system for a stylized multi-entity, multi-currency payments business.
Synthetic merchant flows in ten currencies create exposure across four legal entities;
the system measures that exposure (VaR, Expected Shortfall, stress), forecasts it with
prediction intervals, and chooses forward-hedge ratios that minimise carry cost for a
fixed risk budget, then backtests the policy out of sample and writes the decision report.
All market data is public and cached in the repository; no real company's figures are
used or implied.

> **The deliverable is [`reports/decision_report.md`](reports/decision_report.md).** It
> is regenerated by every run from the committed market-data cache; CI runs every stage
> from that cache with no network access and no API key.

## The question this repository answers

*For a fixed Expected Shortfall budget at the 21-day horizon, what hedge ratios by
currency minimise expected hedging cost, and how would that policy have performed out of
sample from 2015 to the present, including March 2020 and 2022?*

**Headline answer (as of 2026-10-30; the live numbers are in the
[decision report](reports/decision_report.md), which is regenerated by every run):** with
a budget of USD 4m, hedge EUR, GBP, CAD, JPY, CHF, AUD and SGD in full, BRL 69%, and
leave MXN and INR unhedged. Five of those full hedges *earn* carry because the US policy
rate is above the foreign one; together with AUD, which costs 0.7% a year, the
programme earns about USD 0.4m a year in carry against USD 0.3m of bid-ask, while cutting
the 21-day 99% ES of the forecast book from USD 12.9m to the USD 4m budget. The budget is
spent where risk reduction is expensive: BRL at 9.7% a year, MXN at 2.7% and INR at 1.6%.

**Out of sample, 2015 to 2026, monthly rebalance, 142 months
(`reports/policy_backtest.md`):** every policy loses money in total, and that is the
market, not a bug. The business is structurally long foreign currency (it holds merchant
receivables in all ten), and the dollar rose against most of them from 2015, BRL and INR
above all: the unhedged book lost USD 23.8m on currency moves, 18.9m of it in those two.
Hedging decides how much of that loss is kept and what it costs to avoid.

| policy | P&L vol (USD m / yr) | total FX P&L (USD m) | hedging cost per year (+ = cost) | max drawdown | months losing > USD 4m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| unhedged | 10.0 | -23.8 | 0.0 | -36.3 | 12 |
| legacy 50% | 5.2 | -22.3 | 1.2 | -26.1 | 1 |
| full hedge | 2.3 | -20.8 | 2.5 | -22.5 | 0 |
| optimizer, USD 4m ES budget | 4.6 | -6.6 | -0.9 | -18.5 | 1 |

A full hedge removes three-quarters of the FX P&L volatility at a cost of USD 2.5m a year
in carry and spread. The optimizer keeps about half of it, earns USD 0.9m a year instead,
and finished USD 14m ahead of the full hedge over the period, **a lead that is not
statistically distinguishable from zero** (Newey-West p = 0.34). It is made of USD 40m
more carry and spread, which is known when the forwards are dealt, less USD 26m lost on
spot in the high-yielding currencies it left open (BRL, MXN, INR), which is not. The case
for the optimizer is that it takes carry within a stated risk budget, not that it has
shown a P&L edge. Its monthly loss exceeded its ex-ante 99% VaR in 5 of 142 months
against about 1.4 expected (binomial p = 0.015): the budget covers the risk of the hedged
*forecast* exposure, not forecast error or intra-month exposure swings, so it is a
looser bound on realised monthly loss than it looks. March 2020 was the worst month,
USD 5.0m against an ex-ante ES of 3.7m. The frontier figure (`reports/figures/hedge_frontier.png`) shows the
cost of any other budget, and the report's last section lists what the model does not
know.

**What the risk backtests say (`reports/var_backtest.md`):** the risk model is chosen on
2012-2014 by a joint VaR/ES score (FZ0) and tested only on later data. Student-t with
EWMA(0.94) covariance wins; Student-t with GARCH(1,1) volatilities is second and not
distinguishable from it (Diebold-Mariano p = 0.77). Daily from 2015, no model passes every
test at 99%. The chosen model gets the exception count right (32 against 30 expected,
Kupiec p = 0.66, Basel green) and the size of tail losses right (Acerbi-Szekely p = 0.18),
but its exceptions cluster (Christoffersen p < 0.01). That rests on three back-to-back
exceptions, two in the week of the November 2016 US election; GARCH clusters slightly
less but still fails. A faster volatility reacts after the first day of a jump, not
before it, so in a stressed week the ES is a looser bound than the headline suggests.

**Exposure forecasts (`reports/forecast_eval.md`):** a calendar-structural regression
with AR(1) errors beats a same-weekday-four-weeks-earlier naive by a skill of 0.26 at one
day and 0.64 at the 21-day policy horizon (median error 6-10% of exposure). That skill is
an upper bound: the regression has the same terms the synthetic flows were generated
with. Its 95% intervals cover 84-87% of outcomes, so they are somewhat too narrow; the
calibration plot shows this rather than hiding it.

## Architecture

```
                 public APIs (BoC, FRED, ECB, BIS, StatCan)
                                 |
                          fxrisk fetch  ---->  data/cache/<source>/<id>.csv + manifest.json
                                 |                       (committed; retrieval dates recorded)
                          fxrisk quality ---->  DuckDB rebuilt from cache
                                 |              sql/010..050: raw -> catalogue -> usd_per_fcy
                                 |                            -> reconciliation -> master
                                 |              reports/quality_report.md, reports/alerts.md
                          fxrisk flows    ---->  data/flows.parquet (deterministic, config/flows.yaml)
                          fxrisk exposure ---->  sql/060..090: flows -> components -> waterfall
                          fxrisk forecast ---->  forecast_results/eval/latest; reports/forecast_eval.md + figures
                          fxrisk backtest ---->  VaR/ES coverage tests; walk-forward policy backtest; writes the
                                 |              optimizer's monthly positions and rebuilds exposure_group_fx
                          fxrisk hedge    ---->  rates_short, forwards, optimizer; reports/hedge_recommendation.md + frontier
                          fxrisk risk     ---->  risk_results, stress_results; reports/risk_tables.md
                                 v
                          fxrisk report   ---->  reports/decision_report.md (reads the tables above; computes nothing)

   dags/fxrisk_daily.py            one Airflow task per stage, same order (illustrative)
   .github/workflows/ci.yml        every stage from cache, no network, on every push and PR
   .github/workflows/refresh.yml   weekly: re-download, rerun, open a pull request with the new cache and reports
```

| Layer | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| Loaders and cache | `fx_treasury_risk/data/` | one thin loader per source, CSV cache with provenance manifest, offline guard, quality checks |
| Flows | `fx_treasury_risk/flows/`, `config/flows.yaml` | deterministic synthetic merchant flows; annual seasonality calibrated to public retail sales |
| SQL | `sql/*.sql` | every transformation, loaded by file name; each file states what it builds and why |
| Exposure | `sql/060-090`, `fx_treasury_risk/exposure/` | running-sum positions by component, USD translation, gross / net / net-of-hedges waterfall |
| Risk | `fx_treasury_risk/risk/` | returns, sample / EWMA / Ledoit-Wolf covariance, historical / normal / Student-t / t-copula Monte Carlo VaR and ES, stress |
| Forecast | `fx_treasury_risk/forecast/` | seasonal naive and calendar-structural AR(1) exposure forecasts, rolling-origin evaluation, calibration figures |
| Hedge | `fx_treasury_risk/hedge/`, `sql/100_rates_short.sql`, `config/hedge.yaml` | short-rate assembly, CIP forwards and carry, ES-budget optimizer (SLSQP), frontier |
| Backtest | `fx_treasury_risk/backtest/` | risk-model selection on 2012-2014 (FZ0 score, Diebold-Mariano) including a GARCH(1,1) candidate; Kupiec, Christoffersen, conditional coverage, traffic light, Acerbi-Szekely Z1/Z2 from 2015; walk-forward policy backtest with a significance test of the optimizer's lead |
| Report | `fx_treasury_risk/report/`, `sql/110_report_views.sql` | markdown tables, the decision report assembled from persisted tables, exposure figures |
| Operations | `dags/`, `.github/workflows/` | illustrative Airflow DAG; CI; weekly refresh that opens a PR |
| CLI | `fx_treasury_risk/cli.py` | `fxrisk fetch \| quality \| flows \| exposure \| forecast \| hedge \| backtest \| risk \| report \| all` |
| Docs | `docs/` | `methodology.md` (conventions and methods), `hedging-primer.md`, `backlog.md`, `decisions/` (ADRs 0001-0015) |

## How to run

```bash
uv sync --all-groups            # Python 3.12 environment from uv.lock
uv run fxrisk all               # fetch (no-op from cache) -> quality -> flows -> exposure -> forecast -> backtest -> hedge -> risk -> report
uv run pytest                   # 123 tests, no network, about twenty seconds
```

Individual stages run in the same order as `all`; the whole pipeline takes about a
minute and a half on a laptop. Every stage rebuilds what it writes, so a second run of
`all` reproduces the first exactly.

* A `FRED_API_KEY` in the environment or in a git-ignored `.env` switches the FRED
  loader to the official API; without it the public CSV download is used. Neither is
  needed to run from cache.
* `uv run fxrisk fetch --refresh` re-downloads everything; `FXRISK_OFFLINE=1` forbids
  network access (CI sets it).
* `fxrisk quality` exits 1 if any check fails and writes the reasons to `reports/alerts.md`.
* The DuckDB file `data/fxrisk.duckdb` is rebuilt from the cache and can be opened with
  any DuckDB client to inspect every intermediate table and view, including the ones the
  decision report prints.

### Keeping the data current

`.github/workflows/refresh.yml` runs every Monday (and on demand): it re-downloads every
series using the `FRED_API_KEY` repository secret, reruns the pipeline, and opens a pull
request with the updated `data/cache/` and `reports/`. Nothing is pushed to `main`
directly; the PR runs the normal CI and is merged by a person after reading
`reports/alerts.md`. A quality failure still opens the PR, labelled as failed, with the
downstream stages skipped (ADR-0014). The repository needs workflow permissions set to
read and write and "allow GitHub Actions to create pull requests" enabled.

### Reconciliation

USD rates derived from the Bank of Canada's CAD crosses agree with FRED to a median of
2-5 bp (99th percentile 14-56 bp) over 2010-2026; the differences are fixing-time
effects (daily average vs noon). ECB-derived rates differ by 7-35 bp at the median (14:15
CET vs noon NY). Tolerances are set several times above these levels so the check catches
unit and inversion errors, not noise (ADR-0005). Every run prints the full table in
`reports/quality_report.md`.

### Why the quality report says WARN

The only warning is the return-outlier review: 33 daily moves beyond six EWMA standard
deviations since 2010. A statistical filter cannot tell a market event from a bad print,
so each move is checked against the sources the master price did not come from. All 33
are confirmed by at least one independent source and 30 by every one, so they are market
moves, not data errors. Most are named episodes: the Swiss franc floor removal (15 January
2015), Brexit (24 June 2016), the US election (9 November 2016), Brazil's "Joesley Day"
(18 May 2017), the April 2025 tariff selloff. The recent BRL moves (+4.6% on 5 October
2026, -7.7% on 26 October 2026) appear in FRED, the Bank of Canada and the ECB alike. The
three partial confirmations are explained: two are late-day moves the ECB's 14:15 CET
fixing catches a day later, and one (CAD, 6 January 2010) is a 0.4% move that exceeds six
sigma only because it falls on the third day of data, before the volatility estimate
means anything. The table also gives each
move's span in calendar days, so a "daily" move that covers a source holiday (INR on
13 October 2026, four days) is visible as such.

### Why these ten currencies

They span the liquid majors and three emerging-market currencies a payments business
plausibly settles in. Shares of global OTC FX turnover (each trade counts twice, so shares
sum to 200%), from the BIS Triennial Central Bank Survey:

| EUR | JPY | GBP | CHF | AUD | CAD | SGD | INR | MXN | BRL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 28.9 | 16.8 | 10.2 | 6.4 | ~6 | ~6 | ~2 | 1.6* | 1.5* | 0.9* |

2025 survey (April 2025 turnover) except `*` = 2022 survey, Table 3. Used only to
motivate the list; nothing is built on them.

## Conventions (the two that matter)

* **Rates are USD per one unit of foreign currency** (`usd_per_fcy`), for every currency.
  A rising number means the foreign currency strengthened.
* **Positive exposure = long the foreign currency.** The business gains when the currency
  strengthens.

Both are enforced by tests and explained in `docs/methodology.md`.

## The synthetic business in one paragraph

Four entities (US, Canada, euro area, UK) collect card payments in the ten currencies
plus USD, about USD 75m a day in total. Each sale creates a receivable that settles after
one to five business days, merchants are paid daily (US, Canada) or weekly (euro area,
UK), partly in the collected currency and partly after conversion, refunds come back
over the following weeks, retained fees are swept monthly, and the US parent funds the
subsidiaries with quarterly working-capital loans. The net open position in each currency
(receivables plus cash, minus merchant payables, plus intercompany) is the exposure:
USD 7-95m per currency on average, gross up to USD 1bn in EUR. A placeholder legacy
programme hedges half of last month's average net exposure until 2015; from then on the
optimizer's monthly positions replace it in the waterfall and the risk engine. Details in
`docs/methodology.md` sections 3-4 and ADR-0008.

## Limitations and non-goals

* **Synthetic flows.** Volumes, seasonality and settlement lags are stylised and
  calibrated only to public retail-sales seasonality. Nothing here describes a real firm.
* **SGD short rate is a USD proxy** (Singapore has no policy rate; ADR-0015, backlog B1), so SGD
  carry is zero by construction.
* **Policy rates, not OIS.** Forwards are priced off central-bank policy rates (the ECB
  deposit rate for the euro area), so carry ignores rate changes expected within the
  month; one- and three-month OIS rates would be better but are not free (ADR-0015,
  backlog B2).
* **Forwards only; no options, no cross-currency basis, no NDF basis** (hedging primer,
  section 5).
* **Economic exposure, not accounting exposure.** The model hedges the consolidated net
  position per currency. It does not separate transaction exposure (an entity's balances
  in a foreign currency, which hit earnings) from translation exposure (a subsidiary's
  position in its own currency, which hits equity): USD +286m and -165m on 2026-10-30. A
  programme that targets reported earnings would be sized differently (methodology 4.5,
  backlog B21).
* **Fixing-time mismatch** between sources is tolerated, not modelled.
* **Weekday calendar**, no holidays, for settlement and payouts (backlog B9).
* **Coverage tests at one day only.** Ten- and 21-day numbers are reported with a
  caveat; overlapping windows break the tests' independence assumption (ADR-0010).
* **Forecast intervals are about 10% too narrow** (ADR-0011, backlog B16) and the ES
  budget covers only the hedged forecast exposure, not forecast error or intra-month
  exposure swings (ADR-0013).
* **One hedge per month.** Each forward is sized on the average forecast exposure over
  the month it covers, so it under-hedges days above that average and over-hedges the
  month-end dip; an intra-month adjustment rule is backlog B17.
* **Carry is an expected cost** under a random-walk spot; realized hedging cost in the
  backtest includes the spot move (hedging primer, section 3).
* **Stylized transaction costs** (one-way spreads from 0.5 bp to 8 bp) and no
  cross-currency or NDF basis.
* **Not a product:** the Airflow DAG is illustrative and not executed in CI (backlog B7),
  no database beyond a local DuckDB file, no access control.
